In short, Hadoop was created because the internet grew too large for traditional, expensive supercomputers to store and process all the data.
It solved this by distributing massive datasets across clusters of cheap, everyday computers. It achieved this through two main innovations:
HDFS: Splitting huge files and scattering them across multiple machines so no single hard drive gets overwhelmed.
MapReduce: Sending the processing code directly to the machines holding the data, rather than moving petabytes of data across the network to a central processor.
